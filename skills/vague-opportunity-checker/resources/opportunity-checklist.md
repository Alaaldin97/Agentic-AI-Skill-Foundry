# Opportunity Verification Checklist

Use this checklist to evaluate only the information supplied by the user. Do not independently invent or assume facts.

## 1. Organizer identity

Check whether the message provides:

- the legal or public name of the organizer;
- an official website or organizational email;
- the sender's full name and role;
- verifiable contact information;
- a clear explanation of who operates the opportunity.

Missing identity information requires verification but does not alone prove fraud.

## 2. Opportunity structure

Check whether the message explains:

- whether it is employment, an internship, training, volunteering, mentorship, or freelance work;
- responsibilities and expected deliverables;
- hours, duration, location, and start date;
- supervision and reporting structure;
- selection or application steps;
- what happens after the program ends.

## 3. Compensation and costs

Check whether the message states:

- whether the opportunity is paid or unpaid;
- the compensation method when applicable;
- whether the participant must pay any fees;
- whether purchases, deposits, or financial transfers are required.

Requests for payment, banking credentials, passwords, or verification codes are serious warning signs.

## 4. Company and partnership claims

Treat statements such as the following as unverified claims unless evidence is supplied:

- "we work with";
- "connected to";
- "supported by";
- "opportunities with";
- "companies like";
- "our partners include."

Do not transform these phrases into verified employment, sponsorship, recruitment, or partnership relationships.

## 5. Communication and privacy

Check whether:

- communication begins through an identifiable professional channel;
- the sender immediately pushes the user to WhatsApp, Telegram, or another private platform;
- the reason for moving platforms is explained;
- written terms remain available;
- unnecessary personal or financial information is requested.

Moving to a messaging application alone is not proof of fraud, but it may require additional verification.

## 6. Pressure and promises

Look for supported evidence of:

- pressure to decide immediately;
- artificial scarcity or unexplained exclusivity;
- guaranteed employment, income, certification, or acceptance;
- refusal to answer basic questions;
- important details being deliberately postponed;
- inconsistent names, roles, websites, or contact details.

Do not invent urgency or guarantees that are absent from the message.

## Concern labels

### LOW CONCERN

Use when the organizer, opportunity type, structure, compensation, and next steps are sufficiently clear, with no material warning signs in the supplied message.

### NEEDS VERIFICATION

Use when important details are missing or ambiguous, company relationships are unverified, or communication moves quickly to a private platform without enough explanation.

Missing information alone normally belongs in this category.

### HIGH CONCERN

Use only when the supplied material contains serious supported indicators, such as:

- requests for money, banking information, passwords, or verification codes;
- impersonation or materially inconsistent identities;
- guaranteed employment or income combined with pressure;
- refusal to identify the organizer while requesting commitment or sensitive information;
- instructions to conceal communication or bypass normal verification.

Do not use `HIGH CONCERN` merely because the message is vague.

## Question priority

Ask the smallest useful set of questions in this order:

1. Who is the legal or public organizer, and what is its official website?
2. What type of opportunity is this?
3. What are the responsibilities, hours, duration, location, and supervision?
4. Is it paid, unpaid, or fee-based?
5. What is the exact relationship with any companies mentioned?
6. What happens after the program ends?
7. Why must communication move to another platform?

Do not repeat questions already answered clearly.

## Recommended response behavior

The final evaluation should:

- separate confirmed facts from unknowns;
- identify only evidence-supported warning signs;
- assign one proportionate concern label;
- provide prioritized verification questions;
- draft a concise professional reply;
- recommend one clear next action.
